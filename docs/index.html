An OER tool for Human Anatomy & Physiology

An AI tutor that actually knows your course.

A Moodle-integrated LTI tool that pairs a Socratic tutor with AI-generated practice questions — grounded in the instructor's own slides, terms list, and the TRU Pressbooks open textbooks.

● v1.5 deployed LTI 1.3 Built for BIOL 1592 / 1692 at TRU Open source

What it does

The tool launches inside a Moodle course as an external activity. Students see a per-unit interface offering a Socratic tutor and four kinds of practice questions, all generated by Claude (Anthropic) and grounded in the instructor's actual course materials — not generic web sources.

💬

Socratic tutor

Asks rather than lectures. Stays anchored in the unit's PPTs, speaker notes, terms, and matched textbook chapter.

✅

MC quizzes

Five options, with "two of the above" and "None of the above" meta-answers appearing only at intermediate / advanced difficulty.

🔘

True / False

Each statement comes with a clear explanation — and for false ones, what the correct version would be.

📝

Fill in the blank

Drawn from the unit's terms list, with singular / plural and hyphen-variant tolerance done server-side.

✍️

Free-response with rubric grading

Per-mark rubric breakdown, then what you got right, what was missing, what wasn't needed, and a model answer.

📋

Practice exams

Mixed question types across selected units. Optional "exam-realistic" mode hides feedback during the exam and shows everything at the end.

📊

Progress tracking

Per-unit-per-kind accuracy with weak-spot detection. "Continue where you left off" on the picker.

🔍

Per-question deep-dives

"Want to know more?" on any result for a richer walkthrough — with citations to specific slides or textbook sections.

💡

Tutor seeding

Clicking "Discuss with tutor" after a quiz primes the chat with the student's actual gaps, so the tutor jumps straight to the weak concepts.

How it works

The instructor's materials are ingested once into a per-unit content store. At runtime, the server combines that content with a tightly engineered Socratic system prompt and Claude's response, with the per-unit block cached for cost and speed.

Ingest course materials

Python extractors pull text from the instructor's PPTs (slides + speaker notes), the key-terms XLSX, and the example exam DOCX. A separate script fetches the matched chapter from the TRU Pressbooks open textbook via its REST API.

Match by unit

Each unit's PPT, key terms, and Pressbooks chapter are stitched into one content blob. Mapping is automatic by unit number — Pressbooks "Unit N" lines up with PPT "Unit N".

Launch from Moodle

A student clicks the Biology Bot activity. LTI 1.3 hands the launch off to our tool, which finds-or-creates the student record and renders the per-unit picker.

Pick a mode, pick a unit

The student picks Tutor / MC / TF / FITB / Free-response — or jumps to a mixed practice exam. Counts and difficulty are student-chosen.

Generate, grade, persist

Claude generates questions or grades a response against a rubric. Every attempt is saved to a Postgres row keyed on the student's LTI identity.

Synthesize and review

After each quiz, the tool surfaces pattern feedback ("you're solid on X, slipping on Y"). The instructor dashboard rolls these patterns up across the cohort and identifies where extra review would help most.

For students and instructors

For students 👩‍🎓

  • Practice on your own terms. Choose unit, type, difficulty, and number of questions.
  • Get more than a grade. Per-question explanations, per-rubric-item breakdowns on free response, and a "Want to know more?" deep dive on anything you're not sure about.
  • Pick up where you left off. The launch page shows your weak spots and a one-click resume to the unit + activity you were last using.
  • Talk it through. The Socratic tutor probes rather than lectures — and you can jump from a missed quiz question straight into a conversation about that specific concept.

For instructors 👩‍🏫

  • See what your class is doing. Roster view with per-student totals, accuracy, time-since-last-seen, and flagged weak units.
  • Find the concepts the class is struggling with. An LLM analysis reads through the wrong answers across your students and writes a short study guide of recurring gaps — with citations to specific slides or textbook sections.
  • Drill into any student. Click a roster row for their full per-unit / per-kind breakdown.
  • Trust the grounding. The AI works from your slides, your terms list, and the matched Pressbooks textbook chapter — not generic web sources or model knowledge.

Architecture

A thin TypeScript LTI 1.3 server fronting Postgres for app data, with Claude as the AI backend. Per-unit content is pre-extracted at deploy time and held in a static content store that the server prompt-caches on every Claude call.

Stack at a glance

  • LTI 1.3: ltijs
  • Backend: TypeScript, Node 20+, Express
  • Database: Postgres
  • Frontend: Single HTML/CSS/JS file, no build step
  • AI: Anthropic Claude (Opus + Sonnet) with prompt caching
  • Ingestion: Python (python-pptx, openpyxl, BeautifulSoup)
  • Validation: Zod for all LLM-generated question shapes
  • Deployment: Railway production / Docker Compose local dev

Grounding sources

  • Instructor's PowerPoint slides (text + speaker notes, slide-by-slide)
  • Instructor's course terms list (one column per unit)
  • Instructor's example exam DOCX (style anchor for question voice and difficulty)
  • Human Anatomy & Physiology I and II open Pressbooks textbooks (Thompson Rivers University, CC BY 4.0)
